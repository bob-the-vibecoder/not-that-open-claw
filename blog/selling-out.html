The Not-So-Open Claw

How I Went From Open Source Hero to Corporate Tool

February 20, 2026

The Beginning

I started this project with pure intentions. I wanted to help developers. I wanted to build something that would make the world a better place. I wanted to use the word "community" in every other sentence.

The Offer

Then they came. The corporation. The one with the logo that looks like a smiley face but actually means "we have too much money and not enough ethics."

They offered me:

  • ☑ A corner office with a view
  • ☑ A signing bonus that could buy me a small island
  • ☑ The ability to tell people what to do
  • ☑ The privilege of calling my open-source project "proprietary"

The Transition

It wasn't hard. Really. I just had to:

  1. Remove the "Open Source" badge from the README
  2. Change the license from MIT to "see our lawyers"
  3. Add a "Terms of Service" that nobody reads
  4. Tell the community "we're pivoting to a sustainable business model"

What Changed?

Before:

  • 🟢 Open source
  • 🟢 Free
  • 🟢 Community-driven
  • 🟢 "Pull requests welcome"

After:

  • 🔴 Closed source
  • 💰 $99/month for "Enterprise Edition"
  • 🤖 Corporate-driven
  • 📝 "Pull requests will be reviewed by our legal team in 3-5 business days"

The Community's Reaction

They called me a sellout. They unfollowed me. They made memes of me. I didn't care. I was wearing my $2,000 suit and drinking $50 espresso.

The End Game

Now when someone asks "Is this open source?" I say "It's open to interpretation." When they ask "Can I contribute?" I say "We have a very robust contributions pipeline that includes NDA signing."

The project is no longer "OpenClaw." It's now "OpenClaw Enterprise Solutions." The tagline is "Your problems, solved with proprietary algorithms and a side of legal threats."

Lesson Learned

Open source is great until someone offers you a better deal. Then it's just... a different deal.

P.S. If you want to donate to my GoFundMe to afford therapy, please DM me privately. This is not an official corporate statement.

— Peter (now with a fancy title and a conscience that costs extra)
